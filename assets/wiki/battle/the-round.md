# The Battle Round

Battles are simultaneous. Nobody waits their turn: a round is one span of
time in which both sides act at once.

Each round has two halves.

| Half | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Planning | Every side writes orders for as many of its units as it likes. Nothing moves. |
| Resolution | The round plays out in twelve ticks. Everyone drives and shoots together. |

Planning ends for a side when it commits (Enter). Resolution begins once
every side still in the fight has committed, and cannot be interrupted:
whatever you ordered is what your crews will try to do.

## Orders are intents

A unit takes two kinds of order, and they combine:

| Order | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Move | Drive to a tile by the shortest route your side knows |
| Engage | Put a named enemy under fire with a chosen weapon |
| Area fire | Shell a tile whether or not anything is known to be there |
| Hold | Stay put and shoot at whatever shows itself |

A unit with no orders at all still defends itself. Crews do not sit and
watch: any unit with a loaded weapon, a target in range, and line of sight
will fire on its own initiative. Giving no order is a choice about
movement, not about willingness to fight.

Orders can be rewritten freely during planning, and `C` clears a unit's
orders entirely. Once you commit, they are out of your hands until the next
round.

## Ticks

Resolution is twelve ticks long. Within each tick, in order: everyone
moves, then everyone shoots, then scouting is updated, then the battle
checks whether it is over.

That ordering has consequences worth knowing:

- **Speed is time, not just distance.** Movement is spent tick by tick, so
  a fast scout covering four hexes arrives well before a heavy tank
  covering four hexes — and can be somewhere useful while the tank is
  still crossing open ground.
- **Fire is genuinely mutual.** Both shots in a tick land before either
  crew is written off, so two tanks can destroy each other. There is no
  advantage to being the side whose order was written first.
- **Rate of fire matters.** Each weapon reloads on its own clock: a machine
  gun chatters several times a round, an 88 gets a couple of shots away, a
  howitzer manages one.
- **Traffic is real.** One unit per hex, so a column moves at the pace of
  its lead vehicle. A friend in the way is only a moment's delay; an enemy
  in the way ends the advance.

## Reviewing a plan

Before committing, your own units show what they intend: an amber line for
the route each will take and a red line to whatever each will shoot at.
That overlay is the whole point of planning as a separate phase — a round
you can read before you have to live with it.
